# Review round 4 — data validity, model integrity, and runtime audit

Status: **Tiers 1–3 remediated on `feat/data-validity-remediation`; Tier 4 open**

Review date: 2026-08-08. Baseline: commit `11dc27e`.

## Remediation record

Every Tier 1, Tier 2, and Tier 3 finding below is implemented. See
[section 11](#11-remediation-summary) for the per-finding status and the four content decisions
the fixes required. The findings are kept in their original wording so the branch diff can be
read against them.

The headline structural changes:

- **Relationships are now data.** `LevelSpec.relationships` declares conservation, success-rate,
  and burn-rate links between metrics, and the validation gate enforces them across every state
  a participant can reach — the four presets, all 55 option outcomes, and the capstone sets.
- **Option outcomes are authored, not interpolated.** `OPTION_OUTCOME_MODELS` and the
  `value + (after − value) × progress` blend are deleted. All 55 standard options carry explicit
  `metricEffects`; constructing an option without them throws at module load.
- **Preset centers are authored wherever a relationship exists**, and flagged `flat` where the
  quantity is structural or per-request. A level with no variance declares
  `presetsUnavailable` and renders an explanation instead of an inert control.
- **The validation gate exists.** `tests/content-validation.test.mjs` implements all twelve
  checks from [section 10](#10-appendix--validation-gate-specification) and runs in `npm test`.

## Scope and method

This audit covers metric data validity, the outcome model, decision flow, scoring, usability,
and accessibility. It deliberately does **not** re-audit content pedagogy, which
[review-round-3.md](review-round-3.md) already found sound.

Evidence used:

- Static review of `data/levels.ts`, `lib/workshop.ts`, `components/*`, `app/globals.css`, and tests.
- A programmatic audit over the exported level data checking conservation between paired
  metrics, preset variance, physical floors, threshold satisfaction of canonical outcomes,
  canonical-option coverage, evidence-role distribution, and option-kind distribution.
- A live run at `http://localhost:3000` in the in-app browser: full Level 1 submission and
  debrief, a non-canonical Level 2 submission and debrief, and Level 12 capstone inspection.
  Every runtime claim below was reproduced in that session.
- `npm test` (16 pass), `npm run lint` (clean), `npx tsc --noEmit` (clean).

Finding types follow round 3:

- **Verified defect:** reproduced from code or the rendered application.
- **Specification gap:** implemented behavior differs from an accepted requirement.
- **Content decision:** the authored facts are insufficient or contradictory; an author must
  choose a value rather than let a formula infer one.

## Executive conclusion

The curriculum and the decision loop are strong and should not change. The pilot's remaining
risk is **not** missing features — it is that the simulated telemetry contradicts itself in
ways a senior participant will notice on a shared screen, and that one debrief control
misrepresents the team's own result.

Two classes of problem dominate:

1. **Preset centers are generated by a formula that has no knowledge of relationships between
   metrics.** It therefore produces states where completed work exceeds offered work, where a
   ratio falls below its physical floor, and where per-request or structural quantities scale
   with traffic.
2. **Non-canonical option outcomes are linearly interpolated toward the canonical option's
   result.** Blending toward a mechanism the participant did not choose is not a causal model,
   and it makes trap options look like they partially work.

Both were correctly predicted as risks by round 3 (`R3-P0-1`, `R3-P0-2`). Round 3 removed the
runtime fallbacks; this round shows the replacement formula still generates invalid states.

## 1. Verified strengths

These behaviors are correct and should gain regression coverage rather than change.

1. **Level 2 is internally consistent to a professional standard.** 32 completed RPS ×
   483 queries × 3.5 ms per query implies ~54 concurrent database operations against a
   50-connection pool shown at 48 used with 180 ms wait p95. Little's law resolves exactly.
   This is the lab's strongest senior-level moment and should be preserved verbatim.
2. **Level 12's error accounting is exact.** Offered 30/s at 12% errors yields the authored
   26.4/s useful completion; after remediation, 0.7% errors yields the authored 29.8/s.
3. **The canonical ledger closes.** Adopting all twelve reference changes leaves
   €2,370/month and 17 effort points, and no intermediate step goes negative.
4. **Option position is balanced.** The canonical option occupies each of the five slots
   either twice or three times across the eleven standard levels.
5. **Form labelling is correct.** Hypothesis radios, reasoning textareas, and option radios
   all resolve to their visible label text via native label association. Verified in the DOM.
6. **`:fullscreen` presentation sizing exists** ([globals.css:344](../app/globals.css)) and
   materially improves shared-screen legibility.
7. **Reduced motion, forced colors, skip link, focus-visible outlines, `inert` background,
   and dialog focus cycling are all present.**

## 2. Root-cause analysis

### Symptom

Preset and debrief screens display system states that cannot physically occur.

### Five whys

1. Why are impossible values displayed? Preset centers and non-canonical outcomes are computed
   from generic formulas rather than authored.
2. Why are formulas used? Authoring four preset centers × 90 metrics and five outcome sets ×
   11 levels is expensive, so helpers were introduced to fill the gap.
3. Why do the formulas produce invalid states? They operate on one metric at a time and have
   no representation of relationships between metrics — conservation, ratio floors, or
   per-request invariance.
4. Why was this not caught? The test suite asserts that presets *exist* and that outcome
   models change *only authored signals*. It never asserts that a displayed set of metrics is
   mutually consistent.
5. Why did round 3 not close it? Round 3 correctly removed the `after`-leaking fallback and
   documented that the new centers "still require facilitator calibration." That calibration
   was never performed, and nothing fails when it is skipped.

### Root cause

**Relationships between metrics are not represented anywhere in the data model, so no formula
can honor them and no test can check them.** The corrective design is authored values plus a
relationship-aware validation gate — not a better formula.

## 3. P0 findings — correctness and shared-screen trust

### R4-P0-1 — evidence reveals are silently dropped

Type: **verified defect**

[`revealEvidence`](../components/Workshop.tsx) (Workshop.tsx:160-163) reads the closure
variable `revealed` inside the `setState` updater instead of `current.revealed[level.id]`:

```ts
function revealEvidence(id: string) {
  if (revealed.includes(id)) return;
  setState((current) => ({ ...current, revealed: { ...current.revealed, [level.id]: [...revealed, id] } }));
}
```

Two clicks that land in the same React batch both compute their new array from the same stale
`revealed`, so the second overwrites the first.

**Reproduced:** on Level 12, clicking all nine "Inspect evidence" buttons in one batch opened
exactly one card. On Level 2, three rapid clicks opened one additional card.

Human click cadence usually exceeds the batch window, so this is intermittent rather than
constant — which makes it worse to diagnose live.

The same stale-closure pattern governs the hint counters at Workshop.tsx:342 and
Workshop.tsx:373 (`Math.min(2, hintCount + 1)` computed from the closure `hintCount`).

**Fix:** derive from `current` inside the updater in all three places.

**Acceptance:** a test that dispatches N reveals synchronously observes N revealed items.

### R4-P0-2 — the debrief table misrepresents the team's own outcome

Type: **verified defect**

Workshop.tsx:353:

```ts
const teamValue = resultView === "team" ? valueFor(item.id) : item.after;
```

The `Team experiment` / `Recommended reference` segmented control is intended to switch the
**metric cards**. It also rewrites the table's `Team outcome` column, whose header does not
change.

**Reproduced** on Level 2 with the non-canonical "Scale the database" answer:

| Signal | Before | "Team outcome" in team view | "Team outcome" in reference view |
|---|---|---|---|
| Catalogue p95 | 1,840 ms | 553 ms | **190 ms** |
| SQL queries / request | 483 | 483 | **6** |
| Database CPU | 92% | 47% | **34%** |

In reference view both right-hand columns are identical and the team's actual result is
erased. A team that toggles the control to compare is shown a table asserting they achieved
the canonical outcome. Nothing indicates the column changed meaning.

**Fix:** the table always renders the real team outcome. Only `MetricCard` values respond to
`resultView`.

**Acceptance:** toggling `resultView` never changes any cell in the comparison table.

### R4-P0-3 — completed work exceeds offered work

Type: **verified defect and content decision**

`presetCenters` (levels.ts:5-34) derives each metric independently, so paired throughput
metrics diverge. All five cases below are produced by the shipped data:

| Level | Preset | Offered | Completed | |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 12 | No injection | 30 /s | **32.5 /s** | reproduced in the browser |
| 12 | One failure | 30 /s | **30.4 /s** | |
| 2 | Normal | 14 RPS | **44 RPS** | |
| 2 | Campaign | 26 RPS | **41.2 RPS** | |
| 10 | Open sale | 500 accepted | **550 valid outcomes** | contradicts the 500-unit stock invariant |

The Level 12 case is the most exposed: it is the capstone, and "No injection" is the first
preset a participant clicks.

This directly violates [metrics-spec.md](metrics-spec.md) §3 rules 11, 13, and 15.

**Fix:** author the affected preset centers by hand. `usefulCheckout`, `completedRps`,
`useful`, and `accepted` must be authored as a function of their offered counterpart, never
independently.

### R4-P0-4 — a ratio is displayed below its physical floor

Type: **verified defect**

Level 12 at the "No injection" preset renders `Retry amplification: 0.8×`. The metric's own
tooltip definition is "Total downstream attempts divided by original offered requests," which
has a hard floor of 1.0×.

Produced by the generic `direction: "lower"` branch: `min(2.4, 1.2 × 0.65) = 0.78 → 0.8`.

**Fix:** author the four centers for `amplification` (levels.ts:683). A healthy value is
1.0–1.1×, not 0.8×.

**Acceptance:** validation rejects any authored or generated value for a ratio metric that
falls below a declared `floor`.

### R4-P0-5 — the Level 1 preset control does nothing

Type: **verified defect and specification gap**

All eight Level 1 metrics are hardcoded to identical values across all four presets by the
magic-value special cases at levels.ts:7-12. The `Normal / Campaign / Peak / Incident`
segmented control is rendered and interactive but has no effect.

Level 1 is where every participant first touches the interface. A control that visibly does
nothing on first contact costs trust for the remaining eleven levels.

The special cases are also brittle: they match on literal values (`catalogueP95 === 180`,
`checkoutP95 === 420`, `errors === 0.1`), so any future level authored with one of those
values is silently flattened too.

**Fix:** either author real Level 1 centers — the level's own narrative supplies them, since
its canonical outcome is a stepped load test to 31 RPS — or hide the control when a level has
no preset variance. Replace value-matching with an explicit per-metric `presetsFlat: true`
flag.

## 4. P1 findings — model integrity

### R4-P1-1 — non-canonical outcomes are interpolated, not modeled

Type: **verified defect and specification gap**

`optionMetricValue` (workshop.ts:258-264) computes
`value + (after - value) × progress`, where `after` is the **canonical option's** result and
`progress` is a hand-set scalar per option (`OPTION_OUTCOME_MODELS`, levels.ts:129-170).

Blending a participant's chosen mechanism toward a mechanism they did not choose has no
physical meaning, and it produces mutually inconsistent metric sets.

**Reproduced** on Level 2, option `L2-O4` "Scale the database" (`progress: 0.78`):

```
Catalogue p95        1,840 ms → 553 ms
Database CPU             92% → 47%
Pool wait p95         180 ms → 49 ms
DB connections        48/50 → 25/50
Completed work        32 RPS → 32 RPS     (unchanged)
SQL queries / request     483 → 483        (unchanged)
Tested sustainable load   N/A → N/A        (unchanged)
```

Latency falls 70% and database CPU halves, yet completed throughput does not move. That state
cannot occur. Worse, three of the five threshold-bearing signals turn green, so the option the
level exists to argue against appears to substantially work.

Twenty of the fifty-five standard options already carry explicit `metricEffects`. The
remaining thirty-five rely on interpolation.

**Fix:** author `metricEffects` for all options and delete the interpolation branch. The
option data already has the right shape.

**Acceptance:** `OPTION_OUTCOME_MODELS` is empty; validation fails if any standard option
lacks `metricEffects`.

### R4-P1-2 — the canonical option does not reproduce the canonical outcome

Type: **verified defect**

Five levels declare an `after` value for a metric that is absent from the canonical option's
`metricEffects`, so the metric holds its *before* value in the team column:

| Level | Metric | Team outcome | Recommended reference | Source |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2 | Application CPU | 38% | 36% | levels.ts:263 / :281 |
| 3 | Checkout p95 | 520 ms | 500 ms | levels.ts:306 / :322 |
| 4 | Database CPU | 46% | 48% | levels.ts:347 / :362 |
| 5 | Database CPU | 41% | 45% | levels.ts:386 / :404 |
| 7 | Write IOPS | 31% | 32% | levels.ts:473 / :490 |

A team that selects the recommended option is shown a debrief table with rows that disagree
with the recommendation.

**Fix:** for each row, either add the metric to the canonical option's `metricEffects` or set
`after = value` because the change genuinely does not move it.

**Acceptance:** for every standard level, `optionMetricValue(level, canonicalOption, m.id) === m.after`
for all `m`.

### R4-P1-3 — per-request and structural quantities scale with traffic

Type: **verified defect and content decision**

The generic observation branch multiplies by 0.35 / 0.65 / 0.85, which is meaningless for
quantities that are not rates:

| Level | Metric | Normal | Campaign | Peak | Incident |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | SQL queries / request | 13 | 16.4 | 21 | 483 |
| 5 | Healthy instances | **0.4** | 0.7 | 0.9 | 1 |
| 9 | Shared instances | 8.4 | 15.6 | 20.4 | 24 |
| 9 | Checkout instances | 8.4 | 15.6 | 20.4 | 24 |
| 10 | Offered attempts | 7,000 | 13,000 | 17,000 | 20,000 |

Queries per request is per-request and does not vary with arrival rate. Instance counts are
topology, not traffic, and "0.4 healthy instances" is not a state. Only `Offered attempts` is
arguably legitimate.

**Fix:** add a `presetsFlat` flag for structural and per-request metrics, or author the four
centers explicitly.

### R4-P1-4 — the risk band is a single flat multiplier

Type: **specification gap**

`metricStatus` (workshop.ts:146-152) classifies `value ≤ threshold × 1.2` as `risk` for every
`direction: "lower"` metric regardless of kind.

Consequences:

- **Level 7 has no breached metric at any preset.** Primary CPU 91% against an 80% target,
  read IOPS 88% against 80%, and checkout p95 860 ms against 800 ms all land inside the 1.2×
  band. The level's entire board reads amber. The level whose narrative is "the primary is
  overloaded" never turns red.
- The band is simultaneously too tight elsewhere: 1.21% errors against a 1% SLO is `breached`,
  while 960 ms against an 800 ms SLO is only `risk`.

**Fix:** per-kind bands. Utilization and IOPS saturate on a different curve than latency, and
error rates on a different curve again. Declare the band with the threshold in level data.

### R4-P1-5 — scoring does not rank invariant violations lowest

Type: **specification gap**

`scoreBreakdown` (workshop.ts:223-224):

```ts
const interventionFit  = { best: 25, costly: 25, partial: 12, wrong: 0, invariant: 0 };
const costAndSimplicity = { best: 15, costly: 4,  partial: 8,  wrong: 0, invariant: 0 };
```

Two problems:

1. `costly` scores the **same 25/25** on intervention fit as `best`. An over-scaled answer —
   the single most common real-world failure the lab teaches against — totals **79/90 (88%)**.
2. `wrong` and `invariant` both total **50/90 (56%)**. Selecting "Cancel oversold orders and
   reconcile later" (`L10-O5`), which breaches an invariant the briefing calls non-negotiable,
   scores identically to merely targeting the wrong layer, and reads as a near-pass.

The lab's stated thesis is that correctness is never optional. The score model should make
`invariant` the clear floor, below `wrong`.

**Fix:** reduce `costly` intervention fit, and give `invariant` a negative or zero-flooring
term so it cannot reach a passing-looking total.

### R4-P1-6 — reduced motion does not stop the sparklines

Type: **verified defect**

The `prefers-reduced-motion` block ([globals.css:336](../app/globals.css)) suppresses CSS
animation, but the bars are re-seeded by a React state tick every 2,400 ms
(Workshop.tsx:122-126) that ignores the media query. `motionPaused` (Workshop.tsx:60)
defaults to `false` and is manual only.

**Fix:** initialize `motionPaused` from `matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)")` and
subscribe to changes.

## 5. P1 findings — telemetry credibility and usability

### R4-P1-7 — sparklines encode nothing

Type: **specification gap**

`deterministicWave` (workshop.ts:159-166) is seeded by `level.id`, card index, and a tick
counter. It never reads `metric.value`, `metric.after`, or `metric.threshold`. A healthy
180 ms card and a breached 1,840 ms card render statistically identical bars, and a flat
`2 RPS` observation renders a chart that appears to swing between 12% and 94%.

This is [product-improvements-after-feedback.md](product-improvements-after-feedback.md) §5,
previously ranked sixth. Given that the lab's thesis is evidence-based decision making, a
decorative chart that can be mistaken for evidence is the highest-leverage single deletion in
the product. This round recommends promoting it.

**Fix:** replace with an authored before/after transition, or remove the element entirely.

**Acceptance:** every chart encodes an authored value, window, or comparison, and has a text
equivalent.

### R4-P1-8 — the core metric row is a positional slice

Type: **verified defect and specification gap**

`displayedMetrics` (Workshop.tsx:133) is `level.metrics.slice(0, 4)` — the same four metrics
before and after submission.

**Reproduced** on Level 1 after the canonical submission: all four visible cards read
`HEALTHY` (offered 31 RPS observed, catalogue 180 ms, checkout 420 ms, errors 0.1%). The
level's actual finding — application CPU 78% and database CPU 84% at the 31-RPS boundary,
which is the entire setup for Level 2 — is hidden behind "Inspect 4 additional signals."

This also contradicts [metrics-spec.md](metrics-spec.md) §3 rule 18, which requires every
metric to be authored as `core`, `evidence`, or `advanced`.

**Fix:** add the authored tier to `MetricSpec` and allow a level to declare a different core
set before and after submission.

### R4-P1-9 — components that do not yet exist report zero, not N/A

Type: **specification gap**

[metrics-spec.md](metrics-spec.md) §3 rule 19 requires that "values for components that do not
yet exist say `N/A` rather than zero." Three metrics violate it and render as **Breached** at
every preset including Normal:

| Level | Metric | Value | Status shown |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Trace coverage | 0% | Breached |
| 3 | Cache hit ratio | 0% | Breached |
| 11 | Operational partition pruning | 0% | Breached |

There is no cache at Level 3 and no tracing at Level 1, so a red "hit ratio 0%" is a
category error rather than a measurement.

**Fix:** set `value: null` for these three and let the existing `observed` / `N/A` path handle
them, as `sustainableRps` already does at Level 1.

### R4-P1-10 — duplicate accessible names

Type: **verified defect**

Workshop.tsx:317 renders five buttons whose entire accessible name is `Inspect evidence →`
and N checkboxes named `Cite as decisive`, with no reference to the evidence item. Verified in
the accessibility tree.

Form labelling elsewhere is correct — hypothesis radios, option radios, and reasoning
textareas all resolve to their visible label text.

**Fix:** `aria-label={`Inspect ${item.title}`}` and `Cite ${item.title} as decisive`.

### R4-P1-11 — level navigation truncates on a shared screen

Type: **verified defect**

Thirteen tabs at `flex: 0 0 9.5rem` require ~1,976 px against a 1,500 px container, so the
strip scrolls horizontally and titles render as "Unknown capa…", "The campaign …". Verified at
1280 px and 1440 px viewports.

**Fix:** show the number always and the title only for the active tab, or wrap to two rows.

## 6. Cross-level physics — content decisions

Type: **content decision**

These are not defects in code; they are authored numbers that do not reconcile across levels.
A participant who compares two levels can construct each contradiction from displayed values.
Each needs either a corrected number or an explicit in-content explanation.

| # | Levels | Contradiction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 → 2 | Level 1's load test reports **App CPU 78% at 31 RPS**. Level 2 reports **App CPU 38% at 40 RPS** with no application change. Defensible only if the workload mix differs, which is never stated. |
| 2 | 2 → 3 | Level 2 saturates PostgreSQL at ~15,500 queries/s (92% CPU). Level 3 saturates the same database at **7,200 reads/s** (86% CPU) — half the rate, comparable CPU. |
| 3 | 2 (internal) | Level 2's canonical outcome is 6 queries/request at 120 RPS = **720 queries/s at 34% CPU**, against 15,500 queries/s at 92% before. A ~20× non-linearity with no stated cause. |
| 4 | 5 (internal) | One instance at **96% CPU** serves 1,000 RPS; three instances at the same offered load are authored at **45% CPU**. Proportional scaling gives ~32%. |
| 5 | 11 (internal) | 4.2 TB total with 1.6 TB indexes and "78% of rows older than two years" implies a hot set near **910 GB**, not the authored **650 GB**. |
| 6 | 12 (internal) | **22× burn rate** is authored against **12% errors** and a **1% error budget**, which implies **12×**. After remediation, 0.7% errors implies 0.7×, but **0.8×** is authored. |

Item 6 is the cheapest to fix and the most likely to be challenged, because both inputs are
visible on the same card row.

Item 5 is ambiguous rather than wrong — it turns on whether the 650 GB hot set includes
indexes and whether "78% of rows" means 78% of bytes. State the assumption in the level's
evidence text.

## 10. Appendix — validation gate specification

This is [product-improvements-after-feedback.md](product-improvements-after-feedback.md) §4,
made concrete. Every check below detected at least one real defect in this round. It should
run in `npm test` and fail the build.

**Relationship checks** — the class the current suite cannot express:

1. For every declared `(offered, completed)` metric pair, at every preset and in `after`:
   `completed ≤ offered`. *(caught R4-P0-3, 5 cases)*
2. For every metric declaring a `floor` or `ceiling`, no preset center or `after` value
   crosses it. *(caught R4-P0-4)*

### Cross-level physics — the content decisions taken

1. **L1 → L2 application CPU.** Level 1's canonical `appCpu` lowered from 78% to 41%. The
   database saturates first at the 31-RPS boundary, which is what sets up Level 2; a co-saturated
   application muddied that and contradicted Level 2's 38% at a higher load.
2. **L2 → L3 database throughput.** Kept as authored, with a `caveat` on `dbReads` stating that
   catalogue listing reads are join-heavy and cost more per statement than Level 2's point
   lookups. The caveat surfaces in the metric's "i" panel.
3. **L2 internal non-linearity.** Same treatment: a `caveat` on `queries` explains that the
   remediated 6 batched joins each do far more work than one of the 483 lookups.
4. **L5 instance arithmetic.** Canonical `appCpu` lowered from 45% to 34%, which is 96% ÷ 3 plus
   load-balancer overhead.
5. **L11 hot-set arithmetic.** A `caveat` on `hotSet` states the assumption: row age is not
   proportional to bytes, and the 650-GB target is measured after cold partitions and their
   indexes are detached.

### Defects the new gate caught during implementation

The gate found five defects in the remediation itself before any of it was reviewed, which is
the strongest evidence that it earns its place:

1. Capstone aggregation drifted useful completion away from its own error rate under partial
   coverage. Fixed by computing derived metrics from their inputs rather than interpolating them
   independently.
2. `L7-O1` did not author `writeIops`, so the canonical option missed its own outcome.
3. `L5 dbConnections` was authored constant without the `flat` flag.
4. `L4 checkoutP95` and three others breached at the calm preset without declaring why — which
   surfaced a genuine content category, now the `standing` flag: conditions that are breached at
   every preset because traffic is not what causes them (a synchronous provider, distance, 4.2 TB
   of history).
5. `L6 egress` breached at the calm preset because its centers were not proportional to origin
   requests. Both are now derived from a stated 1.41 Mbit per request.
